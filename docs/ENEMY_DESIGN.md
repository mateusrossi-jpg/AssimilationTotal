# AssimilationTotal — Enemy Design

Enemies are one of the most important pillars of the atmosphere.

The goal is not to create generic monsters or zombies.

Enemies must feel:

- assimilated
- biomechanical
- disturbing
- synthetic
- corrupted
- emotionally uncomfortable

The player should constantly feel:

"these were once human."

---

# Core Enemy Philosophy

Enemies should create pressure through:

- silhouettes
- movement
- audio
- swarm behavior
- atmosphere

Not through giant health bars.

---

# Visual Rules

All enemies should follow these rules:

## Shapes

- asymmetrical
- partially broken
- distorted anatomy
- machine-organic fusion

## Materials

- dark metal
- synthetic surfaces
- exposed cables
- glowing cores
- corrupted flesh details

## Lighting

- neon yellow eyes
- unstable glow
- warning red flashes
- electric sparks

---

# Movement Philosophy

Enemies should not move like normal humans.

Movement should feel:

- unstable
- synthetic
- corrupted
- unnatural

Examples:

- twitching
- abrupt turns
- dragging movement
- hovering instability
- mechanical jitter

---

# Enemy Categories — MVP

The MVP only needs 3 enemy types.

---

# Enemy 01 — Assimilated Drone

## Role
Basic swarm enemy.

## Gameplay

- fast
- low health
- aggressive chase

## Visual Direction

- floating surveillance drone
- damaged shell
- neon yellow eye/core
- exposed wires
- unstable hover movement

## Audio Direction

- electronic buzzing
- radio interference
- servo noise
- electrical instability

## Purpose

Creates pressure through numbers.

---

# Enemy 02 — Assimilated Unit

## Role
Medium pressure humanoid enemy.

## Gameplay

- medium speed
- medium health
- more threatening individually

## Visual Direction

- partially human silhouette
- tactical remains
- machine limbs
- distorted posture
- glowing implants
- damaged armor

## Horror Element

The player should still recognize traces of humanity.

## Audio Direction

- distorted breathing
- broken voice fragments
- synthetic whispers
- metal movement

---

# Enemy 03 — Biomechanical Crawler

## Role
Close-range pressure enemy.

## Gameplay

- low profile
- aggressive movement
- dangerous in groups

## Visual Direction

- crawling corrupted body
- multiple limbs
- mechanical spine
- exposed cables
- unstable animation

## Audio Direction

- metallic scratching
- broken servo movement
- distorted flesh sounds

---

# Boss Design Philosophy

Bosses should feel:

- overwhelming
- intelligent
- industrial
- oppressive
- inhuman

Not just:

- giant HP sponge enemies.

---

# First Boss — Assimilation Warden

## Role
Guardian entity of the AI-controlled industrial sector.

## Gameplay Role

The first major escalation moment.

The player should feel:

"the system noticed me."

---

# Assimilation Warden — Visual Direction

- massive silhouette
- biomechanical humanoid-drone hybrid
- floating heavy body sections
- giant yellow energy core
- industrial armor plating
- exposed cables and biomechanical tissue
- red warning lights
- corrupted AI face display

---

# Assimilation Warden — Audio Direction

- low-frequency industrial hum
- distorted AI voice
- warning sirens
- heavy metallic movement
- unstable radio interference

---

# Assimilation Warden — MVP Attacks

Only 3 attacks for MVP:

## 1. Direct Charge
Fast aggressive movement toward player.

## 2. Radial Pulse
Area attack forcing movement.

## 3. Drone Summon
Summons weaker enemies.

Simple implementation.
Strong presentation.

---

# Enemy Readability Rules

Even in visual chaos:

- enemies must remain readable
- silhouettes must remain identifiable
- attack direction should feel understandable

Good readability is more important than visual complexity.

---

# Spawn Philosophy

The player should feel:

- hunted
- surrounded
- pressured

But not unfairly overwhelmed.

The pacing should escalate naturally.

---

# MVP Enemy Goal

The MVP succeeds if:

- enemies feel atmospheric
- enemies feel threatening
- enemies feel mechanically readable
- enemies reinforce the horror identity
- enemies already feel distinct with simple mechanics

Atmosphere and silhouette matter more than complexity.
